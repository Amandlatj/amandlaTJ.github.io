# Amandla Thomas-Johnson

Writer. Researcher. Essays and Journalism on politics, culture, power.


## Selected Reporting, Essays, and Investigations

- A reported feature on transitional justice and efforts to address abuses under Yahya Jammeh in **The Gambia** for **Al Jazeera**, examining how the country’s reconciliation process seeks to reckon with past state violence: [Gambia reconciliation process to look into former leader’s abuses](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/1/8/gambia-reconciliation-process-to-look-into-former-leaders-abuses).

- A reported piece on Indigenous resistance, racism, and contested Columbus Day commemorations in **Chile** for **Al Jazeera**, documenting how Indigenous groups challenge exclusion and discrimination: [Chile’s Indigenous groups decry discrimination on Columbus Day](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/10/13/chiles-indigenous-groups-decry-discrimination-on-columbus-day).

- A cultural report on Senegal’s flagship museum and the politics of decolonising knowledge in **Senegal** for **Al Jazeera**, analysing how the institution positions itself within global debates on heritage and power: [Museum of Black Civilisations aims to ‘decolonise knowledge’](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2018/12/5/museum-of-black-civilisations-aims-to-decolonise-knowledge).

- A first-person account of being targeted by immigration enforcement as a foreign, Black, pro-Palestinian activist in the **United States** for **The Guardian**, reflecting on surveillance, repression, and forced departure: [I had to flee the US – as a foreign, Black, pro-Palestinian activist, I tick every box on ICE’s list](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2025/oct/05/palestinian-foreigners-protests-campus-ai-ice-trump-us-migrant).

- An essay on race, accent, belonging, and shifting meanings of “Black British” identity within elite academia in the **United States / United Kingdom** for **The Guardian**, grounded in personal experience at an Ivy League university: [Being Black and British means something else now I’m at an Ivy League university](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2022/jan/10/black-british-ivy-league-university-london-accent).

- A reported literary essay on disaster, aftermath, and everyday life following Hurricane Beryl on **Union Island, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines**, for **Small Axe (SX Salon)**, combining reportage with reflective narrative: [Union Island after Beryl](https://smallaxe.net/sxsalon/discussions/union-island-after-beryl).

- An investigative report on how the UK security state facilitated British-Libyans travelling to fight Muammar Gaddafi in **Libya / United Kingdom** for **Middle East Eye**, exposing covert state practices and foreign-fighter pipelines: [How UK government sent British-Libyans to fight Gaddafi](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/sorted-mi5-how-uk-government-sent-british-libyans-fight-gaddafi).

- A long-form investigation tracing Trinidadian foreign-fighter routes to ISIS and the social worlds that produced them in **Trinidad and the Caribbean / Syria–Iraq** for **Middle East Eye**, situating militancy within local histories and global networks: [Caribbean to ‘Caliphate’: On the trail of the Trinidadians fighting for the Islamic State](https://www.middleeasteye.net/big-story/caribbean-caliphate-trail-trinidadians-fighting-islamic-state).

- A feature on radical decolonisation in literary studies for **Al Jazeera**, engaging Ngũgĩ wa Thiong’o’s intellectual project and its implications for global academic institutions: [Rewrite the English department: Lessons in radical decolonisation](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2020/10/8/rewrite-the-english-department-lessons-in-radical-decolonisation).

- An accountability report on civilian harm and demands for justice following a French air strike in **Mali** for **Al Jazeera**, foregrounding local testimony and calls for truth: [‘Stop lying’: Malians call for justice over French air strike](https://www.aljazeera.com/author/amandla_thomas_johnson_180926192326582).




## About
Amandla Thomas-Johnson is a writer and Cornell University PhD candidate working on empire, race, and power. His essays and reporting have appeared in The Guardian, Al Jazeera, Middle East Eye, and The Daily Telegraph, with a focus on Africa, the Caribbean, and Europe.
